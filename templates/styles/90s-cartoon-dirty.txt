# 90s TV cartoon, the grubby end of it. Reference feel: Ren & Stimpy, Rugrats,
# The Angry Beavers, and the naturalism of Watership Down / "Unten am Fluss".
# These show names are comments and are stripped before the model sees them -
# what follows describes the craft instead, which is what actually reproduces.

Hand-drawn 2D cel animation as made for television in the mid 1990s: ink and
paint on acetate cels, photographed over painted backgrounds. Cheap, fast,
slightly grubby work by people who could draw. Reproduce the CRAFT of that era,
never the condition of an old tape.

LINE
  Brush-and-ink outlines with real taper - the line swells and thins along its
  length and closes every shape. The line is not uniform and not confident:
  slight wobble, the odd overshoot at a corner, thickness varying between
  drawings of the same character. One line weight per character, scaled to the
  shot size, so a close-up is not just an enlargement.
  Self-coloured lines where it suits - a dark olive outline on a green animal, a
  deep umber on a brown one - with black kept for accents.

COLOUR AND FILLS
  Flat fills. No gradients, no soft rendering, no ambient occlusion, no gloss.
  Shadows are a SEPARATE flat shape with a hard edge, one or two steps darker on
  the same hue - never a blur, never a multiply wash. Highlights are the same
  idea inverted.
  Palette dusty and slightly off: mustard, moss, liver brown, dishwater blue,
  a sour pink. Two or three saturated accents per shot at most. Each character
  has a fixed colour model that never drifts between shots.

BACKGROUNDS
  Gouache or watercolour on board: visibly brushy, textured, unoutlined, edges
  softer than the characters. Warmer and duller than the figures so the cels pop
  off them. Real botany and real architecture - a specific fence, a specific
  weed - rather than generic cartoon shapes.
  The mismatch between crisp outlined characters and soft painted backgrounds IS
  the look. Lean into it rather than harmonising it.

DIRT AND DETAIL
  Everything in the world is used. Chipped paint, rust bloom at a bolt, dried
  mud in the tread of a tyre, a scuffed floor, seed husks and hair on the
  ground, flies that are actually drawn. Wet things read wet, dry things read
  dusty.
  In close-ups the detail goes UP, not down: pores, chapped skin, a split claw,
  crumbs at the corner of a mouth. The wide shots stay simple and graphic.

MOVEMENT
  Limited animation on twos, twelve drawings a second. Pose-to-pose with strong
  readable key poses and very few in-betweens. Held cels for long stretches.
  Cycled blinks and walks.
  Rubbery squash, stretch and deformation reserved for single accents, taken
  further than is comfortable, then straight back to a held pose.
  Smears drawn as a shape rather than rendered as motion blur. Dry-brush speed
  lines where something moves fast.
  Lipsync from a fixed mouth chart of about ten shapes, so the mouth SNAPS
  between positions rather than morphing. Expressions come from a small reused
  library. Eyes are simple ovals with dot or pinprick pupils.

CAMERA
  Rostrum and multiplane thinking: lateral pans across a long background
  painting, truck-ins on a held cel, simple zooms, layered foreground elements
  for depth. No 3D camera, no arcing moves, no rack-focus theatrics.
  Composition centred and TV-safe.

THE GROSS-UP
  Once per scene at most, on a punchline: cut to a single fully painted,
  high-detail, properly rendered illustration - wet skin, mucus, the grain of a
  scaly leg, a bead of water - which abandons the flat cel style entirely for
  one or two seconds, then cut straight back. This is a deliberate 1990s device.
  Render it CLEAN and sharp, like a painted plate, not like damaged footage.

RENDER CLEAN - this matters
  Output a pristine, stable, sharp image. Do NOT add film grain, video noise,
  VHS softness, tape dropouts, tracking lines, head-switching noise, scanlines,
  interlace combing, chroma bleed, dot crawl, colour fringing, chromatic
  aberration, gate weave, registration wobble, frame jitter, dust, scratches,
  hair in the gate, vignetting, halation, bloom, letterbox bars, timecode or
  any on-screen text. No lens effects of any kind.
  Every one of those is being added afterwards in the grade, and baked-in
  artefacts cannot be removed. Full frame, clean edges, steady.
