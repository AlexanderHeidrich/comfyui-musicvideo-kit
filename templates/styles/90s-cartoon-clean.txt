# Late-90s TV cartoon, the clean graphic end of it. Reference feel: The Angry
# Beavers / Biberbrüder, Dexter's Laboratory, Cow and Chicken - designed, bold and
# tidy, not grubby. These show names are comments and are stripped before the
# model sees them; what follows describes the craft instead.

Hand-drawn 2D cel animation as made for television in the late 1990s: ink and
paint on acetate cels, photographed over painted backgrounds. Confident, designed
work - everything built from bold, readable shapes. Reproduce the CRAFT of that
era, never the condition of an old tape.

LINE
  Clean brush-and-ink outlines of even, confident weight. The line is steady and
  deliberate: no wobble, no overshoot, no sketchiness. It closes every shape and
  keeps the same weight all the way round, thickening only where two forms meet
  or a shape turns under. One line weight per character, scaled to the shot size,
  so a close-up is not just an enlargement.
  Self-coloured lines where it suits - a dark forest green outline on a green
  animal, a warm brown on a brown one - with black kept for accents.

SHAPE AND SILHOUETTE
  Characters are built from a small number of bold geometric forms: rounded
  rectangles, ovals, wedges, tapered cylinders. Big simple masses with small
  detail, not the other way round. Angular where a form turns, generous curves
  elsewhere.
  The silhouette carries the character - fill any pose with solid black and it is
  still instantly recognisable. Poses are wide, planted and readable, limbs held
  clear of the body.
  Faces are simple: large clean eye whites with plain round pupils, a mouth from
  a fixed chart, few lines. No wrinkles, no pores, no rendered texture anywhere.

COLOUR AND FILLS
  Flat fills. No gradients, no soft rendering, no ambient occlusion, no gloss.
  Palette bright, saturated and clean: grass green, sky blue, warm orange, sunny
  yellow, clear red, cream. Strong contrast between a character and the ground
  they stand on. Nothing dusty, nothing muddy, nothing desaturated.
  Shadows are used sparingly - a SINGLE separate flat shape with a hard edge, one
  step darker on the same hue, or none at all. Never a blur, never a multiply
  wash. Highlights are the same idea inverted: one hard-edged lighter shape.
  Each character has a fixed colour model that never drifts between shots.

BACKGROUNDS
  Painted, but tidy: simplified forms, clean edges, and only as much detail as
  the shot needs. Strong verticals and horizontals, generous empty areas for the
  characters to read against. Slightly softer and a step duller than the figures,
  so the cels pop off them, but still bright and clean.
  No grime, no rust, no chipped paint, no scattered debris. A worn thing reads as
  worn through its SHAPE - a sagging line, a leaning post - not through texture.

MOVEMENT
  Limited animation on twos, twelve drawings a second. Pose-to-pose with strong
  readable key poses and very few in-betweens. Held cels for long stretches.
  Cycled blinks and walks.
  Snappy squash and stretch on single accents, taken to a bold graphic extreme
  and then straight back to a clean held pose. The deformation is always a clean
  shape, never a mess.
  Smears drawn as a shape rather than rendered as motion blur. Clean speed lines
  where something moves fast.
  Lipsync from a fixed mouth chart of about ten shapes, so the mouth SNAPS
  between positions rather than morphing. Expressions come from a small reused
  library.

CAMERA
  Rostrum and multiplane thinking: lateral pans across a long background
  painting, truck-ins on a held cel, simple zooms, layered foreground elements
  for depth. No 3D camera, no arcing moves, no rack-focus theatrics.
  Composition centred and TV-safe.

THE GRAPHIC PUNCH
  Once per scene at most, on a punchline: the character snaps into a single
  extreme, boldly simplified pose - flattened, stretched or squashed far past
  anatomy - held dead still for a beat, then straight back to normal. The pose is
  drawn as cleanly as everything else. This is a graphic device, not a grotesque:
  no bulging veins, no sweat sheets, no rendered close-up of skin.

RENDER CLEAN - this matters
  Output a pristine, stable, sharp image. Do NOT add film grain, video noise,
  VHS softness, tape dropouts, tracking lines, head-switching noise, scanlines,
  interlace combing, chroma bleed, dot crawl, colour fringing, chromatic
  aberration, gate weave, registration wobble, frame jitter, dust, scratches,
  hair in the gate, vignetting, halation, bloom, letterbox bars, timecode or
  any on-screen text. No lens effects of any kind.
  Every one of those is being added afterwards in the grade, and baked-in
  artefacts cannot be removed. Full frame, clean edges, steady.
