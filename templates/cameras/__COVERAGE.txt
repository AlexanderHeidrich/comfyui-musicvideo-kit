How to choose the three cameras for a scene.

v1 IS THE DIRECTOR'S SHOT
  If the screenplay says anything about framing, v1 says it too. Full stop.
  `mvkit scenes` pulls those words out of the Drehbuch into the `framing` column
  of scenes.tsv - "bird's eye, straight down", "over the shoulder", "worm's eye,
  at ground level" - and `mvkit build` warns when a scene has a framing there
  that v1 does not use. Do not improve on it, do not average it into something
  safer. It is the one shot that was actually asked for.

v2 AND V3 ARE COVERAGE
  They exist so the scene can be cut, and so a failed generation is not a dead
  scene. Standard practice: never repeat v1's shot size, cross the axis rather
  than nudge it, and give at least one of them something the master cannot hold.

  Pick them from what v1 is:

  v1 close-up / extreme close-up
    v2  the wide that establishes where this is happening and who else is there
    v3  the reverse - what the subject is looking at, or the other character's
        matching close

  v1 wide / totale
    v2  medium on whoever carries the beat, waist or chest up
    v3  the insert: the single object or point of contact the wide cannot show

  v1 over the shoulder
    v2  the answering over-the-shoulder from the other side of the pair
    v3  a clean single - the same character without the foreground shoulder

  v1 bird's eye / top down
    v2  eye level and wide, so the space reads as a place and not a diagram
    v3  close on the detail the overhead view flattened away

  v1 worm's eye / ground level
    v2  the opposite - high and looking down, which inverts who has the power
    v3  close on the point of contact with the ground

  v1 profile / side on
    v2  three-quarter front, which is where faces read best
    v3  extreme close on one feature

  v1 a moving shot
    v2  the same action locked off, as the safety
    v3  close on what the move was travelling toward

RULES THAT APPLY TO ALL THREE
  One dominant camera move per variant, written in the kit's bracket shorthand.
  The build turns it into the natural-English sentence H3 actually reads.
  [Push in] is not [Zoom in]. Do not mix pan with truck or tilt with pedestal.
  See __GLOSSARY.txt for the vocabulary and __README.txt for H3's limits.

  Do not put the framing in the action text. The action is reused byte-for-byte
  by every variant, so a "close-up" written there contradicts two of the three.
  The camera block is the only place framing belongs.

WHERE THEY LIVE
  Per scene, in content.json:

    "5": { "title": "...", "lyrics": "...", "shot1": "...",
           "cameras": { "v1": "[Static shot] Close on ...",
                        "v2": "[Static shot] Wide, eye level ...",
                        "v3": "[Push in] ..." } }

  A scene with no `cameras` key falls back to templates/cameras.txt, which is
  the generic wide/angle/detail set. That fallback is fine for a song with no
  screenplay; it is not fine for one that has one.
