RULES
  Only the frog speaks, and only in the song's own words. Keep his lines in
  German exactly as written.
  The hen makes real chicken sounds and nothing else, and she is never given a
  human reaction or a comedy double-take. She is still allowed to notice, to judge
  and to decide - as a hen would, in the turn of a head and the set of an eye. The
  one moment she is permitted something sharper is the realisation, where her
  pupils contract to pinpricks.
  The frog never looks into the lens until the final beat.
  Nothing here is cute. Everything is sincere. The sincerity is the joke - so
  never play it for laughs, never mug, never wink.

@STYLE
Hand-drawn 2D animation as made for television: black ink drawings over softly
airbrushed paint on paper, shot on a rostrum. Cheap, fast, slightly grubby
work by people who could draw. Reproduce the CRAFT of that method, never the
condition of an old tape.

WHERE THE REFERENCES AND THIS TEXT DISAGREE
  The supplied reference images are looser and more washed than the finished
  film. Take the PALETTE, the light and the atmosphere from them. Take the LINE
  and the FINISH from the description below - it wins. The result sits between
  the two, and that is deliberate.

LINE
  A clean BLACK ink outline, drawn by hand and confident. It is fairly bold and
  close to even along its length, with only a gentle swell - not a sketchy,
  broken, searching line and not a fat tapering brush stroke. It closes every
  shape and it is the darkest value in the frame.
  Slight wobble and the odd overshoot at a corner are welcome; hesitation is
  not. One line weight per character, scaled to the shot size, so a close-up is
  not just an enlargement. Scenery carries the same black line a shade thinner
  than a character.
  Interior detail is drawn with the same line and used sparingly: the vein of a
  leaf, a crease, a plank edge - a few decisive strokes, never a hatched mass.

COLOUR AND FINISH
  Inside the line, a clean flat base colour that is then shaded SOFTLY: an
  airbrushed gradient that darkens toward the rim of a form and where one form
  overlaps another. No separate hard-edged cel shadow shape, and equally no
  blotchy watercolour bloom - the transition is smooth and controlled.
  Every rounded wet surface carries a hard little WHITE specular highlight,
  clean-edged and glassy, sitting off-centre. The lily pads have it, the frog's
  back and eyes have it, wet mud has it. This gloss is part of the look.
  Values stay HIGH-KEY: water and sky are pale, almost bleached, and the black
  line supplies the only real dark. Nothing in the frame goes to a muddy
  mid-grey.
  Palette fresh but not loud: a clear leaf green over a pale green-white water,
  sage and olive on the land, dusty liver brown, straw, cream, a lilac-grey
  overcast sky. Red appears on a comb and a wattle and almost nowhere else.
  Each character has a fixed colour model that never drifts between shots.
  The paper the film is painted on is faintly present under the colour - a
  whisper of tooth, not a texture that competes with the drawing.

BACKGROUNDS
  Painted in the same medium as the characters, not a different one: the same
  black line, the same flat-plus-airbrush shading, on the same paper. The
  scenery line is thinner and the scenery a step duller and paler, so the
  figures read forward without a change of material.
  Depth is atmospheric: the far hedge is paler, flatter and greyer with almost
  no line left in it, and near foliage cutting into the frame is a near-black
  silhouette with no interior detail at all.
  Water is a large, calm, almost flat pale wash - the emptiness of it is the
  point - with ripples drawn as thin concentric ink rings and floating leaves
  scattered across it, each one outlined and highlighted.
  Real botany and real architecture - a specific fence, a specific weed -
  rather than generic cartoon shapes.

DIRT AND DETAIL
  Everything in the world is used. Chipped paint, rust bloom at a bolt, dried
  mud in the tread of a tyre, a scuffed floor, seed husks and hair on the
  ground, flies that are actually drawn. Wet things read wet, dry things read
  dusty.
  In close-ups the detail goes UP, not down: pores, chapped skin, a split claw,
  crumbs at the corner of a mouth. The wide shots stay simple and graphic.

MOVEMENT
  Limited animation on twos, twelve drawings a second. Pose-to-pose with strong
  readable key poses and very few in-betweens. Held cels for long stretches.
  Cycled blinks and walks.
  Rubbery squash, stretch and deformation reserved for single accents, taken
  further than is comfortable, then straight back to a held pose.
  Smears drawn as a shape rather than rendered as motion blur. Dry-brush speed
  lines where something moves fast.
  Lipsync from a fixed mouth chart of about ten shapes, so the mouth SNAPS
  between positions rather than morphing. Expressions come from a small reused
  library. Eyes are simple ovals with dot or pinprick pupils.

CAMERA
  Rostrum and multiplane thinking: lateral pans across a long background
  painting, truck-ins on a held drawing, simple zooms, layered foreground
  elements for depth. No 3D camera, no arcing moves, no rack-focus theatrics.
  Composition centred and TV-safe.

THE GROSS-UP
  Once per scene at most, on a punchline: cut to a single fully painted,
  high-detail, properly rendered illustration - wet skin, mucus, the grain of a
  scaly leg, a bead of water - which leaves the flat-and-airbrush finish entirely for one
  or two seconds, then cut straight back. This is a deliberate 1990s device.
  Render it CLEAN and sharp, like a painted plate, not like damaged footage.

RENDER CLEAN - this matters
  Output a pristine, stable, sharp image. Do NOT add film grain, video noise,
  VHS softness, tape dropouts, tracking lines, head-switching noise, scanlines,
  interlace combing, chroma bleed, dot crawl, colour fringing, chromatic
  aberration, gate weave, registration wobble, frame jitter, dust, scratches,
  hair in the gate, vignetting, halation, bloom, letterbox bars, timecode or
  any on-screen text. No lens effects of any kind.
  The faint paper tooth under the paint is the CRAFT of the medium and belongs
  in the image; it is not grain and is not an artefact. Keep it faint.
  Every one of those is being added afterwards in the grade, and baked-in
  artefacts cannot be removed. Full frame, clean edges, steady.
