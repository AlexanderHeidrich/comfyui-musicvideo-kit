The farm lies still around its pond. The house, the barn, the chicken coop and the fenced yard sit in about forty metres of ground, and at the bottom corner of it is one small farm pond, barely three metres across, its green water skinned with duckweed. From straight overhead the chicken-wire fence reads as a thin dark line ruled right around the yard and the coop as a small rectangle of ridged tin, and neither of them is ever missing from the plan. In the water there is at first only a small dark shape, no bigger against the property than a seed. It resolves into a common pond frog - eight centimetres from nose to rump, the length of a man's thumb, forty of his own body lengths from the far bank. He kicks both back legs out and together in a slow swimming stroke and jerks forward on each push, gliding to a stop between kicks, the way a frog actually moves through water. His head stays above the surface and leaves a small spreading wake. He covers half the distance to the bank. Nothing else on the farm moves. At the bottom corner of the pond, no bigger than a seed himself, the crown of a straw hat and the shoulders under it sit motionless on a folding chair, seen from straight above, so there is nothing of the man but the hat brim and his shoulders, with the shadow of the chair beside them.
