FEDERPHIBIEN - MIDJOURNEY PROMPTS FOR THE REFERENCE IMAGES
==========================================================================

Save each result into this folder under the filename in its heading.

Generate them IN THIS ORDER. The frog and the hen are the Federphibium taken
apart, so make that one first and keep it on screen for the other two - that is
what makes the three look related.

Add your own --v flag; the version changes too often to hard-code here.

THE LOOK - THE SHEETS ON DISK ARE THE AUTHORITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Not this text, and not the Drehbuch's header. The Drehbuch offers two directions,
"90er Jahre Cartoon (Biber Bruder, Doug, Ren & Stimpy), eher rau" and "Unten am
Fluss"; the sheets are the second one, and that is the decision. Nothing in this
film is zany. If a word below and a sheet disagree, the sheet is right and the
word gets corrected - which has already happened twice: the text used to claim
gouache and "a dry ragged brush contour of clearly varying weight", and the
sheets have neither. They are transparent watercolour under an even pen line.

Pen and watercolour on cold-press paper, the tooth of the paper visible through
every wash. A fine, even dark brown ink line drawn with a pen and not a brush,
holding its weight all the way round, with dense short hatching strokes following
the direction of feather or form wherever something is shaded. Inside the line,
loose transparent washes that pool and grade softly, a little uneven, sometimes
drifting past it. One soft pale highlight on each rounded wet surface, never a
hard white dot. Warm muted palette: sage and olive green, warm ochre and tan,
cream and bone, brick red only on a comb, pale blue-grey. Values sit in the
middle - nothing is pure white, nothing is pure black. Background a soft neutral
grey-green wash, empty, with the faintest suggestion of a ground plane and a soft
shadow beneath the figure. Children's picture-book illustration, calm and
unhurried - not television cel animation, not vector, not digital.

FOUR VIEWS PER CHARACTER - AND WHY THE LAYOUT KEEPS FAILING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The old character prompts made ONE portrait, front on. H3 had therefore never
seen the frog's back, and every shot written "from behind the frog" came back as
a side-on total instead - it drew the only view it had. Each character now gets
front, side, back and top on one sheet: one picture slot, not four, and the form
both published H3 guides recommend.

Midjourney is bad at this and you will fight it. It has no persistent 3D model of
the character - every view is a fresh interpretation of the same words, and its
training data is overwhelmingly front-facing, so back and top views drift the
most. Expect to hand-pick from 10-20 generations rather than keeping the first.

**Aspect ratio is what controls the spacing.** Views that overlap or drift apart
are a ratio problem, not a wording problem: the pixel budget is fixed, so the
frame shape decides how much room each panel gets. Work it out from the figure:

V8 goes up to 14:1, so a wide row is available - the 2:1 ceiling you read about
elsewhere is a V7 limit. Match the layout to the shape of the animal, because the
pixel budget is constant and each panel should be the shape of what stands in it:

  - The frog and the Federphibium SIT SQUAT and are wider than they are tall.
    One row of four at --ar 4:3, which is what produced the frog sheet. The
    arithmetic here said otherwise - four squat figures in a row would want
    about 5:1, so they should go in a 2x2 grid instead - and the frog sheet
    disproves it: Midjourney simply sizes the figures down to fit the row and
    leaves the space above and below them empty. Both sheets on disk are now
    rows of four at this ratio. An earlier Federphibium came back as a 2x2 grid
    and read perfectly well too, so a good grid is worth keeping if you get one
    - the ratio is what matters, not the arrangement.
  - The hen and the researcher STAND and are taller than they are wide, so each
    panel wants more height. One row of four at --ar 5:2 - the classic
    turnaround strip. Untested: the frog is the only one confirmed so far.

Both give each view a full quarter of the frame, which is as many pixels per view
as one generation can offer.

These prompts are written for MIDJOURNEY V8. Three things differ from every
character-sheet guide you will find online, because almost all of them are V7:

  --style raw  does not exist in V8. It is  --raw .
  --cref       does not exist in V8 at all. It was a V6 / Niji 6 control.
  --q          does not exist in V8 either.

--oref (Omni Reference) is the usual answer to the missing --cref, but it
silently runs your prompt in V7 and you lose V8 rendering entirely. Do not use it
here. On V8 consistency comes from stacking what V8 does support:

  --sref <url of the current sheet>   --sw 100   holds the watercolour look
  --seed <fixed number>                          reproducible starting point
  Personalization (V8.2)                         closest thing to --cref
  identical wording between the four sheets      the cheapest lever of all

Upload the current PNG somewhere to get a URL for --sref. Raise --sw toward 300
if the look drifts, lower it toward 50 if the reference starts dictating the pose
instead of the paint.

If the layout still will not behave, stop fighting it: render each view as its
own image with the same --cref and the same --seed, changing only the view name,
then paste the four together in any image editor. That is the workflow the
turnaround guides actually recommend, and the composite is what H3 reads.

Rules that matter more than they look:
  - NO TEXT, no labels, no view names, no arrows, no height bars. H3 draws
    whatever is in the picture, lettering included.
  - No second character for scale comparison, for the same reason. Scale is
    handled in the prompt text, where it already works.
  - Keep the background an empty soft wash. A wall, a floor or a horizon gets
    read as an environment and carried into the shots.
  - Say the layout explicitly - "one horizontal row of four evenly spaced panels
    of equal width, not overlapping". Vague sheets are where drift starts.

WIRING THEM IN COMFYUI
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do NOT load every sheet into every scene. A connected reference turns up on
screen whether the prompt asks for it or not: with all sheets wired in, a scene
that said "do not use <Picture 5>" still got it as its first frame, and the pond
sheet is why a proposal written for the henhouse kept happening in a puddle.
Connect only the sheets a scene actually contains.

That is what the set-NN_* folders are for. Each is a group of scenes needing the
same sheets, and `mvkit workflows` writes it a graph with exactly those loaders
connected. The folder name lists the sheet numbers; __READ_ME.txt lists them in
the order the loaders must be wired.

H3 numbers <Picture n> over the slots actually connected, so a gap shifts every
tag after it. Within one set, wire the loaders in the order that set names them,
starting at ref_image_0, leaving no slot empty in between.

Set `ref_image_size` on the H3 node to `max`, not `match`. With four views on one
sheet, `match` scales each view down to roughly 270 px and H3 starts guessing.

WHAT STILL HAS TO BE DRAWN BY HAND - CHATGPT PROMPTS
==========================================================================

The six sheets above cover the four characters and the two places. They do not
cover a prop that appears in no sheet, a creature that appears only as a baby,
lettering, or two bodies touching - and those are exactly the shots H3 will
invent badly. Nine images are worth making before the first render.

Make them in ChatGPT (GPT Image), not in Midjourney: it takes an uploaded sheet
as a style reference in the same conversation, it holds a described layout, and
it can write legible handwriting - which is the whole point of two of them.
There are no --flags; say the aspect ratio and the "no text" rule in words.

Open the conversation by uploading 02_char_der-frosch.png and 03_char_das-huhn.png
and saying: "This is the style and these are the characters. Match the paper, the
even brown pen line, the transparent washes and the palette exactly in
everything that follows." Then paste one prompt per image.

TWO KINDS, AND THEY ARE WIRED DIFFERENTLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A SHEET (A1-A5) is a <Subject n> like every other sheet: parts on bare paper,
never a finished view, and it goes into scenes.json's `sheets` list for the
scenes that contain it. Adding one regroups the sets, so re-run `mvkit pack`
and `mvkit workflows` afterwards.

A FIRST FRAME (B1-B4) is the one legitimate standalone <Picture n> in this kit.
It is a finished 16:9 frame, it is wired first, and the prompt that uses it must
switch its summary from [reference generation] to [keyframe completion] and add
a standalone <Picture n> entry naming it as the first frame. That scene then has
a sheet tuple of its own and gets its own set folder. Do not wire one into a set
that other scenes share - a connected picture turns up whether the prompt asks
for it or not, and this one is a frame.

Note while you are in here: the 05_ and 06_ prompts above still describe a
"background painting". The sheets on disk are element boards, which is what the
kit wants; the wording never caught up. Do not regenerate them from that text.
