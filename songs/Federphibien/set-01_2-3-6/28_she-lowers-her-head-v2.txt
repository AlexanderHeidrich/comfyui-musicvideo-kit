subject_definitions
<Subject 1> is THE FROG, shown in <Picture 1>: a common European pond frog, 8 cm nose to rump
and 10 cm sitting up - the length of a man's thumb, and he fits in a cupped human
hand. He sits squat and is wider than he is tall, with a big round pale cream
belly. Grey-green skin, not warty, with soft olive mottling. Large white eyes set
high and wide on top of the skull, each with a small black dot pupil, domed above
the skull line so they break his silhouette from every angle. A wide mouth drawn
as one long calm curve. Damp, with a soft pale highlight where the light catches
him. Introverted, hopelessly in love, entirely sincere.

<Subject 2> is THE HEN, shown in <Picture 2>: an ordinary plump farmyard hen, 45 cm to the top of
her comb and 50 cm beak to tail - four to five times the frog's height. Standing
beside her he reaches her hock and no higher, and she must fold her neck right to
the ground to bring her beak to him. She stands on two thin scaly yellow-orange
legs, body carried fairly upright in a soft pear shape, breast full and rounded,
the tail carried up and fanned into a short point. Warm tan and ochre plumage
throughout, the folded wing picked out in fine dark hatching rather than a
darker colour, and a pale cream breast and belly. Upright brick-red comb, small dark red wattle,
short orange-yellow beak, the same white eyes with dot pupils as the frog. No
eyelashes, no blush, no anthropomorphism. Noble, haughty, indifferent.

<Subject 3> is the hen house interior, built from the separate element
studies in <Picture 3> - a sheet of parts on bare paper, not a view of the
place, so its layout is never reproduced: tall walls of green-painted vertical timber boarding framed by heavy
warm-brown posts and beams, one horizontal rail across the wall at a third of its
height, an ochre bare-earth floor, a big heap of grey-white down banked against
one wall with loose feathers on the floor and drifting in the air, a straw
nesting shelf high under the roof with eggs on it, two black cables strung across
with one bare bulb hanging off a sagging one, and a doorway in a
brown timber frame filled with flat blown-out daylight. Palette here: green
timber, warm brown, ochre earth, grey-white down. Never corrugated tin inside,
never water, never a pond. 3.5 m to the ridge, so the wall runs far above the hen
and the hen far above the frog.

detailed_description
Pen and watercolour on cold-press paper, in the manner of a children's picture
book. A fine, even dark brown ink line drawn with a pen and not a brush, holding
its weight all the way round, with dense short hatching strokes following the
direction of feather or form wherever something is shaded. Inside the line, loose
transparent washes that pool and grade softly and sometimes drift past it, the
tooth of the paper showing through every one of them. One soft pale highlight on
each rounded wet surface, never a hard white dot. A soft cast shadow under
anything standing on the ground. Warm muted palette: grey-green and olive, warm
tan and ochre, cream and bone, brick red only on a comb. Values sit in the
middle: nothing is pure white, nothing is pure black. Calm and unhurried, never
zany, never television cel animation and never vector. Limited animation on twos
with strong held poses.
Output clean and sharp: no film grain, video noise, VHS softness, scanlines,
chroma bleed, gate weave, dust, vignetting, bloom, lens effects or on-screen
text.

The scene is inside <Subject 3>, the hen house: green-painted timber boarding, heavy warm-brown posts, an ochre earth floor, the heap of grey-white down banked against one wall, one bare bulb on a sagging cable, and the doorway filled with flat blown-out daylight. There is no water in this room - no pond, no puddle, nothing wet on the floor.

Screen geometry, the same in every shot inside the hen house: the frog is on the right, low in the heap of down, and the hen stands over him on the left, so the two of them face each other across the picture. The bright doorway is out of frame past the frog on the right, and anything leaving for it crosses from left to right. From behind the frog she stands centred beyond him; from in front of him the doorway lies centred beyond him.

overall_soundscape
A working summer farm, quiet and constant under everything: wet plops, crickets,
the low mutter of hens off frame, corrugated tin ticking in the heat, one distant
tractor that never arrives. Flies. No cartoon sound effects of any kind - no
boings, no slide whistles, no comedy impacts. Any croak or cluck is
recorded-real, never performed.
