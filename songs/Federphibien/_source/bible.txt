REFERENCE - read this first
  Seven images are supplied. Four are subjects, one is the look, two are places.

  <Picture 1> THE FEDERPHIBIUM, the hybrid child: a fat green toad body with a
  red chicken comb, a red wattle under the chin, a small yellow-orange beak, huge
  orange-ringed eyes set high and wide, a pale cream belly, dark green speckling,
  frog legs with long toes ending in small yellow claws, and two small useless
  white-and-green feathered wings on its flanks.
  <Picture 2> DER FROSCH.  <Picture 3> DAS HUHN.
  <Picture 5> DER FORSCHER, seen from behind, which is how he is always seen.

  <Picture 4> is NOT a thing in the scene. It is a colour and construction board:
  the palette, the line weight, and the way the shading grades inside the line. Read the look off it and never draw it.
  <Picture 6> is the pond, <Picture 7> the inside of the hen house. They are
  background paintings that set the colour and the architecture of those places.
  Neither is a character and nothing in them is a subject.

  Take from the character references: the design, the proportions, the colour
  model, the exact shape of comb, wattle, beak, eyes, wings and claws, and the
  palette and light they are painted in.
  Do NOT copy how loosely they are drawn. The references are washier and sketchier
  than the finished film. On screen the outline is a clean bold BLACK line, the
  colour inside it is a flat base softly airbrushed darker toward the rim, and
  every wet rounded surface carries one hard white glint. Tighten them to that.
  What is NOT part of the film is the staging of the sheet: each figure stands on
  a plain pale backdrop with a cast shadow under it. Drop the backdrop and that
  shadow and put the character in the scene. Never reproduce lettering, caption,
  logo, watermark or frame from any reference.

CAST - the reference is the child, and the parents are it taken apart
  DER FROSCH - <Picture 2>. The narrator, and the only one who ever speaks. Green
  warty skin, dark speckling, a pale cream belly, huge orange-ringed eyes, long
  clawed toes, a potbellied sitting build, a wide frog mouth. He is what
  <Picture 1> is with every chicken part taken away: NO comb, NO wattle, NO beak,
  NO wings. Permanently wet, with a
  visible sheen. He wears nothing and owns nothing. Earnest, lovesick, entirely
  without shame.
  DAS HUHN - <Picture 3>. A plain brown farmyard hen carrying exactly the chicken parts that
  <Picture 1> borrowed: the same red comb, the same red wattle, the same
  yellow-orange beak, the same scaly legs and claws. Otherwise completely
  ordinary and completely oblivious. She never once deliberately looks at him -
  everything that reads as a response is coincidence, she was pecking anyway,
  she turned her head anyway. Beady black eyes. No eyelashes, no blush, no
  anthropomorphism of any kind.
  DIE FEDERPHIBIEN - the imagined children: <Picture 1> exactly, in numbers.
  Comb, wattle, beak, frog body, stub wings, clawed webbed feet. The wings
  cannot lift them and they will not swim, and they do not know it yet. They
  appear ONLY inside the chorus fantasies, never in the real pond.
  DER FORSCHER - <Picture 5>. The only human being in the film, and the film never shows his
  face. Khaki trousers, a plain shirt with the sleeves turned back, a straw hat.
  A hardbound notebook and a pencil, and an SLR on a black strap round his neck -
  most of the time only the strap is visible, the body and lens of the camera
  only in the close shots of the notebook and at the very end when he finally
  uses it. Middle aged, unhurried, completely absorbed. He sits on a folding
  chair at the edge of the pond and draws what he sees, in the fine pencil manner
  of a nineteenth century scientific plate, the parts labelled in a small hand.
  Always seen from behind or over his shoulder. Never his face, never a reaction
  shot, never a word. He does not interact with the animals and they never notice
  him. He moves quickly exactly once, when he jumps up and grabs the chair to
  stop it going over.

WORLD
  A murky farm pond at the bottom edge of a chicken yard. High summer, humid,
  overcast bright. Green scum water, cattails, duckweed. A leaning chicken-wire
  fence with a gap worn under it. A corrugated tin coop. Dry mud cracked into
  plates. One bare bulb on a sagging cable. Flies everywhere.
  The farm plainly works - the coop is repaired, there is feed on the ground -
  and nobody is home. The one person who ever appears is the researcher at the
  pond, and he is never shown from the front. Never explain either of those.

SCALE - these are real animals and they are not the same size
  The references are portraits: every character is drawn alone, filling its own
  frame, on a plain backdrop. Nothing in them says how big anything is. Size
  comes from this list and from nowhere else. Never size a character by how much
  of its reference image it fills.

  DER FROSCH      a common European pond frog. 8 cm from nose to rump, about
                  10 cm sitting up. The length of a man's thumb. He fits inside
                  a cupped human hand with room to spare.
  DIE FEDERPHIBIEN  the same frog body sitting upright like a meerkat, about
                  15 cm to the top of the comb. Still a small animal.
  DAS HUHN        an ordinary farmyard hen. 45 cm to the top of the comb,
                  50 cm beak to tail. Four to five times the frog's height.
                  Standing beside her he reaches her hock - the backward joint
                  above her foot - and no higher. To bring her beak to him she
                  must fold her neck right down to the ground.
  DER FORSCHER    an adult man, 180 cm standing, his eye about 120 cm up when
                  he is sitting on the folding chair. Eighteen times the frog,
                  four times the hen. The hen comes to just below his knee. The
                  frog would sit on his palm.
  A FLY           8 mm. A tenth of the frog's body length, a speck beside him.

  PLACES, to the same ruler
    The pond is a farm pond, about 10 m across - the frog needs a couple of
    hundred of his own body lengths to cross it, and the whole of his journey
    from the water to the hen house is roughly 2 m of ground.
    The farmyard is about 40 m across and holds the house, the barn, the coop,
    the fenced run and the pond. Seen from the air the pond is one small dark
    oval in the corner of it and the frog in the water is a single dark speck.
    The hen house is a shed a man can stand up in - about 2 m to the ridge -
    with a chicken pop-hole about 35 cm square and a battened plank ladder up
    to it. Inside, the hen is a small animal against a big wall, and the frog
    is small against the hen.

  HOW THIS SHOWS ON SCREEN
    In any frame holding both the frog and the hen, she is at least four times
    his height, whatever the angle - a low shot may exaggerate her, none may
    shrink her.
    In any frame holding both the frog and the researcher, the frog is at most
    a twentieth of the man's height.
    In any frame holding the whole farm, the frog is a dark mark of a few
    pixels, not a readable animal. He only becomes readable when the framing is
    tight enough to hold the pond alone or less.
    A close-up enlarges a character in the frame. It never enlarges the animal:
    the things around it - grass blades, gravel, feathers, a corn kernel - grow
    with it in the same proportion.

RUNNING GAGS
  The frog is always wet. Wherever he sits a small dark puddle forms, and it is
  still there after he moves away.
  Flies orbit his head in every single beat. He never eats one. Not once.
  One single corn kernel keeps coming back - dropped, carried, offered, ignored.
  The hen never blinks at the same time as he does.
  Hearts appear exactly twice in the whole film and nowhere else: rising over the
  frog's head when the focus comes back to him, and circling the pair as they
  clear the hen house door. Both times they are flat hard-edged cartoon shapes,
  drawn like everything else, held a moment and gone. Never a constant, never
  throbbing, never a drifting background of them. Everywhere else the film
  refuses that shorthand.

RULES
  Only the frog speaks, and only in the song's own words. Keep his lines in
  German exactly as written.
  The hen makes real chicken sounds and nothing else. She is never given a human
  reaction, never a take, never a double-take.
  The frog never looks into the lens until the final beat.
  Nothing here is cute. Everything is sincere. The sincerity is the joke - so
  never play it for laughs, never mug, never wink.
